The landscape

Investment teams often use agents to research markets, summarize diligence materials, analyze financials, monitor portfolio companies, and prepare investment committee materials.

The same tools may serve multiple funds and deals, making hard walls between deals essential. Access must also change quickly as employees, advisors, and diligence partners enter or leave a process.

The stakes

Industry challenges

  1. 01The same analysts and tools work five live deals at once — one broad search can cross a wall.
  2. 02Advisors and diligence partners rotate weekly, and their access rarely ends when the engagement does.
  3. 03A reusable research agent becomes a bridge between confidential processes unless something actually separates them.
  4. 04When a leak is suspected, you need to show exactly who and what touched the data room — most firms can’t.
Capabilities

How Fabriq helps investment firms

Isolate agents by fund, deal, portfolio company, and data room.Each engagement is its own island: an agent on one deal has no path into another’s data room, even on shared tooling.
Keep competing or confidential deals walled off from each other.Work on competing targets stays separated by the system itself, not by analyst discipline alone.
Limit external advisors to the scope and duration of their engagement.Advisor access is limited to the deal and time-limited to the engagement, ending automatically when the work does.
Control whether an agent may summarize, export, share, or modify source material.Reading and summarizing can be allowed while export and external sharing stay blocked — per document set, per deal.
Require approval before distributing investment materials externally.Anything addressed to an LP, target, or counterparty holds for a partner or IR review before it leaves the firm.
Record which sources and agent actions contributed to an investment artifact.Every memo and model traces to the documents and analysis that informed it — provenance for the IC and for diligence defense.
