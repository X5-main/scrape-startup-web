            Phase 1: Think Like a Product Manager First<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

            Timeline: Week 1 (Days 1-5)<\/strong><\/p>\n\n\n\n

            Here\u2019s where most people mess up, they rush to build the agent. DON\u2019T. <\/p>\n\n\n\n

            This week is about thinking like a product manager, understanding what investors actually<\/em> ask before due diligence so your agent can handle those questions confidently.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

            Step 1: Map the Question Landscape<\/h4>\n\n\n\n

            Work with your CFO\/Financial lead to dig into past conversations: emails, recorded calls, meeting notes. Categorize questions by theme:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

              \n
            • Financials (ARR, MRR, burn rate, runway)<\/li>\n\n\n\n
            • Market & Competition (TAM, competitive landscape, differentiation)<\/li>\n\n\n\n
            • Operations & Team (org structure, key hires, culture)<\/li>\n\n\n\n
            • Product & Sales (product roadmap, customer acquisition, retention)<\/li>\n\n\n\n
            • Vision & Strategy (long-term goals, exit strategy)<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

              If you\u2019re fundraising for the first time: <\/strong>Start simple. Use YC\u2019s standard investor question list, talk to a few founders who\u2019ve recently raised, and anticipate questions from your pitch deck. Aim for 20\u201330 FAQs to start, expand as new ones come in.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

              Step 2: Build Your FAQ Bible<\/h4>\n\n\n\n

              Each FAQ should do four things:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

                \n
              1. Answer the question clearly<\/strong> \u2013 provide the exact number or fact.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
              2. Add context<\/strong> \u2013 explain the why<\/em> behind the answer.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
              3. Show proof<\/strong> \u2013 include data, charts, or benchmarks.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
              4. Set guardrails<\/strong> \u2013 define what the agent can or can\u2019t disclose and how it should handle off-limits queries.<\/li>\n<\/ol>\n\n\n
