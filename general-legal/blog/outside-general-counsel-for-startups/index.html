Outside General Counsel for Startups: What to expect?

General Legal Team
August 13, 2026

An outside general counsel (OGC) is an attorney or law firm that provides ongoing legal guidance without the cost of a full-time in-house legal team.

Demand for this model is growing, with 48% of chief legal officers now relying on external support. After years of pushing legal work in-house to cut costs, rising litigation, increasing regulatory scrutiny, and understaffed legal teams have reversed that trend.

But where does that leave startups?

Many founders still view ongoing legal support as something reserved for larger companies with bigger budgets. As startups navigate tighter funding environments, leaner teams, and increasing legal obligations, that assumption might be worth revisiting.

To help you evaluate whether this model makes sense for your company, this guide covers everything you need to know about outside general counsel for startups.

Key takeaways

  • Outside general counsel provides ongoing legal support without a full-time hire
    Startups gain access to experienced legal guidance without building an in-house legal team.
  • Many startups need OGC sooner than expected
    Fundraising, hiring, contracts, and expansion often create legal needs long before a full-time GC makes sense.
  • OGC supports every stage of growth
    Common responsibilities include fundraising, governance, contracts, employment, IP, privacy, and compliance.
  • The model is often more cost-effective
    Founders get senior legal expertise without the salary, benefits, and overhead of an internal legal department.
  • AI-native firms make the model even stronger
    Firms like General Legal combine attorney expertise with technology to deliver faster service, predictable pricing, and startup-friendly support.

What is outside general counsel?

Outside general counsel is a legal arrangement in which an attorney or law firm functions as your company’s general counsel without joining the payroll.

For startups, this arrangement typically follows three stages:

  • Understanding the company’s business model, operations, risk profile, and growth plans
  • Establishing the legal foundations the company needs
  • Remaining involved as the business evolves

Unlike traditional outside counsel, which is often hired for a specific transaction or dispute, OGC serves as a consistent legal point of contact across the organization, working directly with founders and leadership teams.

When does a startup need outside general counsel?

A startup may look for outside general counsel when its legal needs become too frequent, complex, or business-critical to handle on an ad hoc basis.

This counsel becomes especially valuable when your startup is:

  • Raising capital regularly: Multiple funding rounds require consistent corporate governance, investor documentation, and securities compliance.
  • Signing more contracts: As the number of customer, vendor, and partnership agreements increases, founders often need faster and more consistent legal review.
  • Growing headcount: Hiring employees, issuing equity, and implementing workplace policies create new legal obligations.
  • Expanding into new markets: Operating across multiple states or countries introduces additional regulatory and compliance requirements.
  • Building in a regulated industry: FinTech, AI, healthcare, life sciences, crypto, and other regulated sectors face legal questions that arise long before startups can justify a full legal department.
  • Preparing for an acquisition or exit: Clean corporate records, contracts, and compliance processes can make diligence significantly smoother.

A useful rule of thumb: If legal issues are becoming a recurring part of running the business rather than a one-off event, it may be time to consider outside general counsel.

How does outside general counsel compare to in-house counsel?

The biggest difference is that in-house counsel is an employee of the company, while outside general counsel is an external attorney or law firm that provides ongoing legal support. Both perform many of the same functions, but outside general counsel offers additional capacity without requiring a full-time hire.

However, the two models aren’t mutually exclusive.

Many growing companies use outside general counsel alongside an in-house legal team. This is often a matter of necessity, as companies with less than $1 billion in revenue have a median legal team size of just two lawyers and three total legal staff members.

While in-house counsel focuses on the company’s daily legal needs, outside general counsel can provide specialized expertise, support major transactions, and help manage periods of increased workload. This allows internal teams to stay focused on the business while still having access to additional legal resources when needed.

For startups, however, the comparison is often more straightforward.

Most early-stage companies don’t have enough legal work to justify hiring a full-time general counsel. Outside general counsel fills that gap by providing consistent legal support until the business reaches the scale where building an internal legal team makes sense.

But what does that legal support actually entail?

What startups can expect from outside general counsel: 7 core service areas

A startup can expect outside general counsel to provide strategic legal leadership that supports the business as it grows. While the scope varies by company, most engagements center around the following seven core areas of support.

1. Fundraising and equity management

With investors pouring $300 billion into startups globally in the first quarter of 2026 alone, founders are navigating an increasingly active fundraising environment. That’s exactly why startups benefit from having experienced legal guidance throughout the fundraising lifecycle.

An outside general counsel can help:

  • Structure and review SAFEs, convertible notes, and priced financing rounds
  • Negotiate and analyze term sheets before founders commit to key terms
  • Manage cap table issues, equity grants, and dilution considerations
  • Ensure compliance with applicable securities laws and filing requirements
  • Coordinate corporate approvals and documentation required for fundraising
  • Prepare the company for investor due diligence and future financing rounds

Just as importantly, they help founders understand how today’s fundraising decisions can affect ownership, control, and future financing options.

2. Entity formation and governance

Founders spend a lot of time building products and raising capital, often overlooking the legal foundation supporting both. This is a mistake, as many of the issues that slow down fundraising, diligence, and acquisitions can be traced back to foundational legal decisions made years earlier.

An outside general counsel can ensure the company is built on a solid legal footing by:

  • Advising on the most appropriate entity structure for the business
  • Drafting and maintaining bylaws, operating agreements, and other governing documents
  • Documenting founder ownership, equity allocations, and vesting arrangements
  • Preparing board resolutions, written consents, and meeting minutes
  • Maintaining corporate records and governance frameworks that support fundraising, due diligence, and future exits

3. Contract review and negotiation

Startups run on contracts. From customer agreements and vendor relationships to partnerships and software licensing, nearly every stage of growth involves negotiating legal terms that can affect revenue, liability, and operational flexibility.

An outside general counsel can ensure these agreements protect the business while keeping deals moving by:

  • Drafting, reviewing, and negotiating NDAs
  • Managing customer agreements, MSAs, statements of work, and SaaS contracts
  • Reviewing vendor, supplier, and procurement agreements
  • Negotiating partnership, reseller, referral, and integration agreements

Beyond individual agreements, outside general counsel can also develop standardized contract templates, approval workflows, and negotiation playbooks that reduce legal bottlenecks and support faster growth.

4. Employment and HR compliance

Hiring sounds straightforward until startups start adding employees, contractors, advisors, and remote workers across multiple states. 

Misclassification, poorly drafted employment agreements, and missing IP assignment provisions can create legal, tax, and ownership issues that often surface during fundraising, due diligence, or disputes with current and former workers.

An outside general counsel can ensure your workforce is structured and documented correctly by:

  • Drafting offer letters, employment agreements, and onboarding documentation
  • Preparing consultant, independent contractor, and advisor agreements
  • Advising on employee versus contractor classification
  • Drafting confidentiality, invention assignment, and IP ownership agreements
  • Developing employee handbooks, workplace policies, and compliance procedures
  • Managing equity grants, incentive plans, separation agreements, and other employment-related documentation

5. Intellectual property protection

For many startups, intellectual property (IP) is the business. Yet ownership and licensing issues often remain hidden until an investor, acquirer, or opposing party starts asking difficult questions. 

That complexity is one reason IP is among the most frequently outsourced legal functions in smaller companies: 74% of legal departments use outside counsel for intellectual property matters to some degree.

An outside general counsel can protect and strengthen your IP position by:

  • Ensuring founders, employees, and contractors properly assign IP to the company
  • Reviewing invention assignment, confidentiality, and NDA agreements
  • Identifying potential ownership, licensing, and third-party IP risks early
  • Coordinating trademark, copyright, and patent filings with specialized IP counsel
  • Monitoring open-source software usage and related compliance obligations
  • Aligning the company’s IP strategy with its fundraising, growth, and exit objectives

6. Data privacy and regulatory compliance

Most startups collect, store, or process customer data long before they have dedicated compliance resources. As privacy regulations continue to expand, staying compliant requires more than publishing a singular privacy policy and moving on.

An outside general counsel can manage privacy and compliance obligations by:

  • Drafting privacy policies, cookie notices, and data processing agreements
  • Advising on GDPR, CCPA/CPRA, and other applicable privacy requirements
  • Developing internal data governance and data handling policies
  • Supporting AI governance, vendor management, and incident response planning
  • Coordinating HIPAA-related documentation and other industry-specific compliance requirements

7. Strategic legal guidance and risk management

Without proactive legal guidance, startups often end up operating in a reactive mode: addressing issues only after they become urgent, expensive, or investor-facing.

An outside general counsel can help avoid this scenario by:

  • Assessing legal risks before new initiatives, partnerships, or expansions
  • Advising on growth strategies, governance decisions, and operational changes
  • Monitoring regulatory and compliance developments that may affect the business
  • Coordinating specialized outside counsel when niche expertise is required
  • Managing disputes, demand letters, and emerging legal issues
  • Aligning legal decisions with the company’s long-term fundraising and exit objectives

Given that proactive risk management is limited in 65% of legal teams due to resource constraints, outside general counsel can be a cost-effective way for startups to access the strategic legal oversight needed to stay ahead of emerging risks.

Cost-effective access to experienced legal counsel is one of the main reasons startups choose the outside general counsel model. But it’s far from the only advantage.

Why hire outside general counsel for your startup: 5 key benefits

Hiring outside general counsel gives startups several advantages, including these five key ones:

  • Reduced founder workload: Startup founders already wear enough hats. OGC takes ownership of day-to-day legal matters, allowing you to focus on product, customers, and growth rather than contracts, compliance questions, and legal administration.
  • Specialized expertise on demand: Few startups need a full legal department, but most eventually need advice on fundraising, employment, contracts, intellectual property, privacy, and compliance. OGC provides access to that expertise as it becomes relevant, without the cost of building a team of specialists.
  • Embedded legal partnership: Re-explaining your business to a new lawyer every time an issue arises is inefficient. OGC develops institutional knowledge of the company over time, enabling faster guidance and advice that reflects your business model, investors, and long-term goals.
  • A more holistic approach to growth: Legal challenges rarely exist in isolation. Modern legal leaders routinely oversee compliance, privacy, ethics, and risk management alongside traditional legal work. OGC brings that broader perspective to startups, helping legal strategy evolve alongside the business.
  • Cost efficiency: Building an internal legal function is expensive, particularly for companies still searching for product-market fit or scaling headcount. OGC provides access to senior legal expertise without the salary, benefits, equity, and overhead of a full-time hire, making legal support easier to scale as the company grows.

Avoiding the cost of a full-time legal hire can sound appealing to startups. But what kind of investment does the OGC model require?

How much does outside general counsel cost?

The cost of outside general counsel varies based on the startup’s stage, legal complexity, and preferred engagement model. Most startups encounter one of the following arrangements:

Engagement model How it works How much it usually costs
Hourly billing The law firm bills for the time spent on each matter, with rates varying based on attorney seniority, experience, and firm size. - $300-$500/hour for associates
- $600-$1,000+/hour for partners and senior counsel (elite firms may exceed $2,000-$4,000/hour)
Monthly retainer The startup pays a fixed monthly fee in exchange for ongoing legal support, strategic guidance, and access to counsel for day-to-day legal matters. $1,500-$15,000+ per month
Quarterly retainer The company engages counsel on a quarterly basis for periodic legal reviews, strategic planning, and a defined scope of ongoing support. Varies by scope; typically lower than a full monthly retainer
Project-based pricing Legal services are provided for a specific transaction, initiative, or deliverable, with pricing agreed upon before the work begins. $1,500-$5,000+ per project, with larger transactions costing substantially more
Hybrid model The company pays a recurring retainer for routine legal support while separately paying fixed fees for major transactions or specialized projects. Retainer fees plus project-based pricing as needed

The legal industry still overwhelmingly runs on billable hours, with 90% of legal spending flowing through this model. However, AI-native law firms are beginning to challenge the status quo by offering fixed-fee pricing that provides startups with greater predictability and cost control.

That’s just one of the reasons these firms are becoming a popular choice for outside general counsel.
