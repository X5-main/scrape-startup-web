Most behavioral health networks are directories — not care systems.

An invisible quality gap

While claims indicate who delivered care, they fail to show which providers are available, whcih treatments are working, or when members need additional support.

A fragmented member journey

Therapy, psychiatry, primary care, and specialty care remain disconnected — leaving members to navigate referrals and transitions themselves.

A total-cost problem

Members with behavioral health conditions are 27% of the population but 56.5% of total healthcare costs — most of it outside behavioral health services.

From increasing volume to increasing value

01

Customized, evidence-based care pathways

Every patient enters through a comprehensive evaluation, to identify the specific interventions most likely to improve outcomes, combined and adjusted over time.

02

One care team, nothing duplicated

Psychiatry, therapy, and diagnostics work together from one record and one care plan, and treatment stays aligned as it evolves. Changing level of care is a warm handoff, not a referral out. No repeated intakes, no duplicated assessments, no one lost between providers.

03

Outcomes measured, not assumed

Progress is tracked from intake to discharge using validated measures, and every plan is reviewed against the data at least every four weeks. Alongside protocol-specific measures, this includes functional transdiagnostic measures. When a patient is not improving, the pathway changes. Improvement is reported, not asserted.
