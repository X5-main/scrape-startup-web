## This week in AI

### 1 . Can India build its AI future on borrowed models?

Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Mukesh Ambani have both called for India to become an AI superpower. But as Indian companies continue to rely on frontier models developed by OpenAI, Google, Meta, and Anthropic, the debate is shifting from AI adoption to AI sovereignty.

### 2 . Should AI companies prepare people for the jobs they'll replace?

OpenAI and Anthropic have joined a new $500 million initiative aimed at helping workers adapt to AI-driven changes in the job market through retraining, career coaching, and workforce programs.

### 3 . Is AI changing cybersecurity?

AI-powered cyber threats are pushing Indian enterprises to rethink how they protect their systems. As attacks become more sophisticated, companies are shifting cybersecurity spending towards identity protection, AI governance and cloud security.

## Our take

### Does India Need Its Own ChatGPT?

India's AI ambitions are hard to miss. The government wants the country to become an AI superpower, while companies like Reliance are investing heavily in data centres and positioning themselves as India's "AI backbone". But much of that ecosystem still depends on models built by OpenAI, Google, Meta, and Anthropic.

There is nothing wrong with building on global technology. It helps companies move faster and bring AI to millions of users. But the question still remains: can a country truly become an AI leader if it depends on models developed elsewhere? As frontier AI becomes more strategically important, owning the underlying technology could matter just as much as building applications on top of it.

India has the talent and one of the world's largest AI user bases. The next challenge is deciding whether it wants to remain one of the biggest adopters of AI or become one of its creators.
