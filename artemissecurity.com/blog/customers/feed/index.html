About Lemonade

Lemonade is the AI-powered insurance company, that has rebuilt insurance around automation and machine learning from the ground up. Its environment is large and busy, spanning cloud infrastructure, identity systems, AI agents, and data warehouses, and it changes continuously as the company ships. Protecting an environment of this size and pace is achieved by a security team that wanted tooling as modern as the company itself, led by CISO Jonathan Jaffe.

The opportunity

For Lemonade, the core security challenge at this scale comes down to understanding the environment itself. With 500,000+ of assets across cloud, identity, and data, the team needed a clear and current picture of what it had, what normal looked like, and where real risk was concentrated. Alerts and logs alone left too much of that picture to manual effort, and the work of assembling it manually grew heavier as the environment expanded. What Lemonade wanted was continuous intelligence about its environment that stayed accurate on its own as things changed.

“With our rapid adoption of agentic technology, our environment evolves daily. I wanted a security platform that could keep an accurate picture of it without my team rebuilding that picture by hand.”

Jonathan Jaffe  ·  CISO, Lemonade

The challenge

Lemonade already ran an established SIEM, and it did the job it was built for by collecting logs and raising alerts. As the company grew, the team spent real effort turning that raw telemetry into a working understanding of the environment, and that effort grew with every new asset and account. Investigations were also a distributed effort. The team coordinated over Slack and Zoom, worked separate tools in parallel, and consolidated what each person found by hand into tools such as Slack canvas or Notion pages, which left the reasoning behind an investigation spread across individual tools rather than held in one place. They wanted depth and context they could trust, delivered in a form the whole team would rely on daily, through the calm periods as much as the busy ones. 

One conventional way to get that kind of always-on depth is to hire a managed service, and the team knew the tradeoffs that come with one: quality that varies with whoever picks up the case, and context that lives outside the company. Lemonade wanted the coverage without the compromise. Coming from a company that cares deeply about customer experience, Lemonade expected the same high bar in their security products.

The solution

Artemis gives Lemonade that experience and understanding through its environment intelligence. Artemis builds a living model of the whole environment and keeps it current as things change, bringing assets, context, and risk together in one continuously updated view. The team can see what they have, how it’s connected, and where attention is most warranted, all in one place.

“The most important thing is that everything works together, in one place. Artemis ingests any logs we throw at it, automatically creates detectors based on current threat intel, our environment, and the logs themselves, and then does deep analysis of what it finds. Each piece matters, but all of it together is what makes the product.”

Jonathan Jaffe  ·  CISO, Lemonade

Detection coverage builds itself on top of that model. Artemis ingests any log source Lemonade sends it, then automatically creates detectors from three inputs working together: current threat intelligence, Lemonade’s own environment, and the logs already flowing in. When new threats emerge or the environment shifts, coverage follows on its own, without the team writing and maintaining rules by hand.

AI mode lets anyone on the team ask questions about the environment in plain language, and get answers grounded in Lemonade’s own data. Agentic investigations run across the connected sources and work cases on their own, around the clock, applying the same depth to the first case of the day and the fiftieth. Shared case management gives the team a common place to set verdicts and track status together.

How Lemonade uses Artemis

Day to day, environment intelligence has become one of the features Jonathan reaches for most. He navigates it to understand Lemonade’s assets, see the context around them, and catch what has changed across the environment. That model has surfaced more than security findings: Artemis flagged cost and security posture optimization opportunities across Lemonade’s cloud, data, and identity infrastructure.

“I love Artemis’ optimization insights. Almost immediately, Artemis made us aware of ways to optimize key, expensive resources like Snowflake instance, EKS clusters, and Okta in ways that lowered our operational costs of those things. We never would have discovered those optimizations on our own”

Jonathan Jaffe  ·  CISO, Lemonade

AI mode sees similarly heavy use, with the team running hundreds of conversations to ask questions and get grounded answers about their own security data. Cases flow through a single shared workspace where the team triages, investigates, sets verdicts, and moves work forward together. Across a large asset base, this gives them a steady way to focus on the risk that genuinely matters and to keep that focus current as the environment grows.

“As a team, we spend multiple hours in Artemis a day. I might spend an hour in the product every day, myself. I look forward to using Artemis each morning.”

Jonathan Jaffe  ·  CISO, Lemonade

Cases arrive already investigated. By the time a team member opens one, a deep analysis is waiting, and the work starts from conclusions and evidence rather than raw alerts. From there, AI mode runs alongside every investigation, which gives the team one place to go deeper on what an investigation turned up and a record of how it got there. An analyst picking up a colleague’s case can see what was already investigated and what came back, confirm nothing was missed, and rerun the same investigation later if the question returns. Work moves through the team along the same path: a team member may hand off a case, hunt, or investigation, to another member, who takes the actions Artemis recommends, reviews the findings, and routes fixes to owners of the resource in question..

The case also carries the checks that used to be assembled query by query. When secret scanning flagged an AWS credential pair, the open question was whether the matching secret key had been exposed anywhere else, in source control or on an endpoint. Answering that once meant hand-crafting endpoint queries, searching repositories manually, and refining until the result was trustworthy enough to report. Artemis now runs that search across the connected sources and returns the evidence behind its answer.

“Before Artemis, an investigation meant Slack, Zoom, and trying to consolidate everyone’s findings into a canvas or a Notion page. Now AI mode sits alongside every investigation, so it all lives in one place with a paper trail. I can see what my coworker was looking for and what he found, and I know no stone was left unturned.”

Nick Kosmas  ·  Security Engineer, Lemonade

Artemis is useful beyond the security team, as well. Lemonade has made AI mode available to IT with limited permissions, so a ticket that needs investigation before anyone changes a configuration, an authentication failure for example, can be worked through directly rather than waiting on security to look first.

Results and impact

Artemis now serves as the full member of Lemonade’s security operations with environment intelligence and AI mode among the most-used parts of the product. Investigations that once took meaningful analyst time now complete in just minutes, with thousands of them run automatically every month.

“It’s like having a service working 24 hours a day that never gets tired, doing what you would normally have to hire a managed service for, with more consistency and higher quality. The time it saves is hard to even measure, because there’s work we simply would not be able to do without it. We have put Artemis to work across our whole environment, and it has kept earning its place. It is one of the few security products I can say is genuinely getting better over time.”

Jonathan Jaffe  ·  CISO, Lemonade

About Cursor

Cursor is one of the most widely used AI coding agents and one of the fastest-growing software companies of all time. Inside Cursor, engineering runs at a blazing pace. Changes ship continuously, and the infrastructure underneath it changes by the hour. Securing an environment that moves this fast falls to a lean, deeply technical security team led by CISO Tom Daniels.

The opportunity

For Daniels, the same shift that lets Cursor ship fast also rewrites the security problem. When code, infrastructure, and identity all move at machine speed, so do the risks. An exposed credential, an over-privileged automation account, or a developer reaching into production can become an incident in seconds, faster than any human analyst can work through an alert queue.

The other half of the equation is the attacker. Frontier AI is increasingly available to adversaries, and AI-orchestrated attacks have already moved from research reports to reality, compressing intrusions from days to minutes. Daniels’s working assumption is simple: AI is available, someone will use it to attack.

“We are an AI company end to end. It did not make sense for security to still operate at human speed.”

Tom Daniels  ·  CISO, Cursor

That led Daniels to a clear conclusion. Detection and response could not simply be assisted by AI. They had to be AI-native: adaptive, self-tuning, and able to investigate on their own.

The challenge

Cursor already had a legacy SIEM in place, but it was built with AI bolted on top rather than at the core of the product. What Daniels wanted was a platform designed for the way an AI-native organization actually operates. Manual detection engineering, where people write and maintain every rule by hand, could not keep up with an environment that never holds still.

The team also had strong views on how the platform should work. They are engineers first, and they wanted control and transparency rather than a black box. That meant keeping ownership of their data, providing context on what matters for detection coverage, bringing their own detection logic, working via MCPs, and always being able to see exactly what the system was doing and why

The solution

Artemis fits the way Cursor operates. Artemis starts by learning the environment: AI agents continuously map Cursor’s systems, identities, and behaviors, building baselines of what is expected: which automation accounts touch production, how developers reach infrastructure, what routine data movement looks like. 

On top of it, instead of a static rule set, Artemis generates adaptive detectors and tunes them automatically as the environment changes, so coverage keeps pace with the org rather than falling behind it. Today, thousands of detectors are continuously tuned to Cursor’s environment and recalibrate as behavior shifts.

Control stayed with Cursor. With a bring your own ClickHouse model, Cursor kept ownership of its data. Security engineers author custom detectors in plain language through AI Mode or Artemis’ MCP, with versioning, audit history, one-click rollback, and GitHub as the source of truth.

Investigations run at machine speed, correlating across sources and working cases autonomously, and every verdict is auditable: the reasoning chain, the queries the agent ran, and the evidence behind each conclusion ship with the case.

Response actions such as suspending a compromised account are generated dynamically and stay behind human-in-the-loop confirmation. Day to day, the team lives in a Slack-native workflow with the ability to tag Artemis to run AI Mode sessions.

“We now use Artemis to generate working detections and find the correlations across log sources automatically. That’s where most of our effort used to go. Now if we want to detect something, we just ask the agent in plain English: help us write a detector to find this.”

Zehuan Li  ·  Security Software Engineer, Cursor

How Cursor uses Artemis

Artemis watches the places where a fast-moving AI company is most exposed. It correlates across more than 20 log sources: cloud audit logs, identity activity, proxy logs, and data access logs, stitching siloed signals into cohesive investigations. It monitors the developers and automation accounts that touch production, break-glass cloud access, and anomalies in the identity provider, and it can catch attempts at machine-speed data exfiltration before they turn into incidents.

On top of that, Artemis hunts. It turns new external intelligence into threat hunts across the whole environment, more than 1,200 hunt executions in the last quarter, and when a hunt hits, it opens a case backed by the same evidence-cited investigation as any detection. When something feels off, the team can also launch its own hunt in plain language through AI Mode, straight from Slack.

For the people who use it every day, Artemis has become the default starting point.

“My browser basically opens to Artemis now. The minute anything looks off, it is the first place I go.”

Zehuan Li  ·  Security Software Engineer, Cursor

Results and impact

Cursor standardized on Artemis as its AI-native detection and response platform across the environment. Moving off its old SIEM onto a single platform put detection, investigation, and response in one place, built for the speed at which the company operates.

The difference shows up in the results: every alert generated or ingested from over 10 billion events a day across Cursor’s environment is investigated automatically, and 96% of them close without human involvement. The security team now focuses their precious time on investigating the handful of decision-grade cases that matter, each arriving pre-investigated with the reasoning and evidence already assembled.

Because the team can tune and iterate on detectors in minutes, coverage and precision keep improving in step with the environment, ahead of what manual processes could sustain.

Underpinning all of it is a close technical partnership, with fast response times and steady delivery on what Cursor asks for.

The strategic outcome is readiness: as attacks themselves become AI-orchestrated, Cursor’s detection, investigation, and response already run at machine speed, and adapt as fast as threats do.

“We are a team that could have built a lot of this ourselves, and it still made more sense to go with Artemis. Doing it in-house would have been slower and more expensive. With Artemis, we have a world class team building for us 24/7 and ability to use Artemis’ MCP for the things we want to build internally.”

Tom Daniels  ·  CISO, Cursor
