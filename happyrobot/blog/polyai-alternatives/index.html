One of the best PolyAI alternatives is HappyRobot, but it depends on how your business looks to automate the processes around phone calls and interactions. PolyAI remains a strong enterprise voice AI platform for inbound customer service, with its deepest results in hospitality and banking. 

One of the major alternatives to PolyAI is HappyRobot because it deploys AI workers and voice AI agents for operational phone work. This means the outbound collections calls and carrier scheduling become the areas where agents must also write the outcome back into systems such as Salesforce or McLeod.

In this piece, we’ll compare both platforms, PolyAI and HappyRobot, to help you determine which one is a better fit for your business.

What Is PolyAI?

PolyAI is an enterprise voice AI platform that answers inbound customer service calls and resolves them without a human agent. The company was founded in London in 2017, and its CEO, Nikola Mrkšić, was the first engineer at VocalIQ, the conversational AI startup Apple acquired to improve Siri.

The product rests on two models built in-house: Raven and Owl.

Raven is the LLM that is trained on more than one billion enterprise customer conversations, which is why it holds business rules better than a general-purpose model would on a two-minute support call. 

Owl is the speech recognition model, fine-tuned on an NVIDIA Riva foundation with PolyAI's own telephony data, because phone audio is noisy and roughly 70% of voice agent errors start at the transcription step. 

Marriott, Caesars, and Golden Nugget use PolyAI in hospitality, MetroBank in banking, and PG&E in utilities. PolyAI has raised over $200 million, including an $86 million Series D in December 2025 with NVIDIA's venture arm participating.

What Is HappyRobot?

HappyRobot deploys AI workers that make and answer business phone calls, then complete the follow-up work inside systems like Salesforce, McLeod, and Gmail.

For instance, the collections call ends with the payment commitment, which gets written into the platform that the Accounts Receivables team uses. A carrier scheduling call concludes with a booked appointment and the record updated in McLeod or Turvo. Also, it's the same workers that handle the email and SMS threads that surround those calls, so the conversation continues across channels without losing its history.

The company went through Y Combinator in 2023 and raised a $44 million Series B after growing revenue tenfold in a year. 

Its earliest customers were freight brokers, and it now runs AI workers that act in real workflows across finance, sales, recruiting, and customer operations. DHL processes hundreds of thousands of emails and millions of voice minutes through HappyRobot annually, and Job&Talent ran more than 100,000 candidate interviews through it, producing over 3,000 hires in a few weeks.

HappyRobot’s deployment happens through Forward Deployed Engineers who build the first workflows with the customer's team and take them to production in weeks.

HappyRobot vs PolyAI: Head-to-Head

HappyRobot and PolyAI both answer business phone calls with voice AI and are capable of holding conversations well enough that you won’t be able to understand the difference in a single demo.

The difference between HappyRobot and PolyAI lies in what each platform does with the call after it is answered.

Here are the six key use cases that drive this distinction based on what the platforms do. 

1. Operational calls vs customer service calls

The platforms differ most in what happens after the caller hangs up the phone because this difference decides whether the platform fits your workload before you compare any features.

  1. PolyAI resolves the conversation

PolyAI is built to resolve the call itself, which is why its case studies measure containment and resolution. It marks the job as complete in the tool once the call ends.

Here’s how it functions:

  • Golden Nugget automates 34% of all calls to its central reservations line, and 87% of its non-reservation requests run fully automated.
  • Hopper has PolyAI fully resolving 15% of its inbound call volume around the clock, with a goal its CX leadership states as resolution over containment.

  1. HappyRobot completes the work that a conversation triggers

HappyRobot deploys AI workers that treat the call as one step inside a longer workflow. A collections conversation produces a payment commitment, and the AI worker writes that commitment into the receivables system and sends the confirmation email in the same run.

Some of the key use cases are as follows:

  • Customers running collections this way report a 119x return on the cost to collect.
  • Circle Logistics cut inbound call volume to its human teams by 80% by automating its sales calls.
  • Kuehne+Nagel handles 78% of connected calls end-to-end across its Air Logistics operations, which raised team capacity by 47%.

2. Voice AI for the call center

HappyRobot and PolyAI can replace queue-based call handling. Which one you pick depends on the type of call your team answers all day.

The deployments below show what a resolved call looks like on each platform.

  1. PolyAI absorbs inbound volume at contact center scale

PolyAI comes with voicebot contact center deployments that can absorb seasonal spikes without hiring. It's the same sort of problem that Golden Nugget bought it to solve, where its call center voice AI holds natural conversation through interruptions and topic changes. 

Also, it is something Gartner reviewers single out as the reason autonomous fulfillment rates were lifted.

  1. HappyRobot handles call center work that is tied to operations

Again, the volume of calls does mimic that of a call center, but the difference is that each resolved call also updates the service and dispatch records behind it.

Encompass Supply Chain Solutions runs its LG appliance service line this way, and the numbers show what that looks like at production volume:

  • Encompass Supply Chain Solutions runs roughly 1,700 LG-related service calls per week through HappyRobot.
  • 64% of those calls resolve without a human touching the ticket, and customer sentiment holds at 93% non-negative.

An AI worker significantly reduces the number of calls that reach human agents, and it also completes the follow-up each call creates, such as the ticket update or the technician dispatch.

3. Deployment speed and effort

Both vendors reach production in weeks, so speed will not decide this one. The real question is who can change the workflow after launch, your team or the vendor's.

  1. PolyAI deploys in about six weeks, vendor-led

PolyAI states on its own technology page that a voice assistant takes about six weeks to build, integrate, and deploy. The build runs through PolyAI's team.

Moreover, Gartner reviewers note that changes after launch route through PolyAI, and that advanced integrations with legacy backend databases still require the vendor's account engineering team.

  1. HappyRobot deploys with Forward Deployed Engineers on-site

Forward Deployed Engineers build the first workflows with your team, which keeps working knowledge of the system inside your company after launch.

Naturgy evaluated close to 30 vendors and took HappyRobot from POC kickoff to production in 3 months on its highest-volume technical service line. The agent scored 9.4 out of 10 in customer satisfaction during its first weeks of operation.

4. Integrations and telephony

Integration reach determines which systems the agent can act inside once the call ends. Take a call that ends with a rescheduled delivery. If the voice agent writes to the scheduling system, the reschedule is done when the call ends. If it cannot, a human reads the transcript and retypes the change manually. 

An integration list tells you which parts of the job stay automated after the phone is answered.

  1. PolyAI connects deeply within the contact center

PolyAI connects through SIP and PSTN and works with Twilio, Azure, Dynamics 365, and Microsoft Teams. One Gartner reviewer describes it as plugging directly into the company CRM to handle booking updates and order management.

Its coverage ends at the contact center's edge, which is consistent with what the product is built for.

  1. HappyRobot connects to business systems beyond the contact center

HappyRobot connects to more than 200 systems across CRM, ERP, ticketing, and data infrastructure, with named integrations including Salesforce, McLeod, Turvo, and Snowflake.

Where a system has no API, HappyRobot uses browser agents with OCR and navigates the system the way a human operator would.

Telephony is bring-your-own. Existing Twilio, Telnyx, or SIP infrastructure keeps working, and your current call center voice services stay in place while AI workers handle the calls inside them.

5. Pricing and transparency

Neither company publishes pricing. PolyAI bills per minute under custom enterprise contracts. A Capterra reviewer says pricing depends on too many factors and fluctuates, and a five-star Gartner reviewer calls the total cost of ownership premium and hard to justify for low-volume operational workflows. 

HappyRobot also remains quote-only, scoped to the workflows deployed rather than minutes on the phone. To get a number, book a HappyRobot demo.

6. Security and compliance

Both PolyAI and HappyRobot have a clear enterprise security review with the certification lists overlapping on SOC 2, GDPR, and HIPAA. The sections below cover the engineering practices a CISO would ask about during vendor assessment.

  1. PolyAI ships SOC 2, HIPAA, GDPR, and PCI DSS as standard

These certifications come from formal audits. Separate from them, PolyAI's own team has shared two engineering practices that show how it protects customer data in daily operation:

  • PolyAI locks each deployment to a fixed version of its transcription model, so an update cannot change how it transcribes a word, like your company's name, and break the workflows built on it.
  • Customer names, addresses, and account numbers stay out of its speech training data. PolyAI uses synthetic data generation instead: an AI model writes sample names and account numbers, and text-to-speech converts them to audio for training.

  1. HappyRobot holds SOC 2, GDPR, and HIPAA, with node-level audit logs

HappyRobot's compliance alignment extends to the EU AI Act, NIST CSF, and DORA. Every action an AI worker takes is logged at the node level, and that log is what makes the platform deployable in regulated industries:

  • A compliance officer can pull any run and see what the AI said on the call and what it wrote into which system.
  • For regulated industries, that audit trail is the difference between a pilot and a production deployment.

Where PolyAI Is the Better Choice

PolyAI is the right call for call-heavy enterprises where the phone line carries high volumes of routine inquiries. A few leading industry use cases for PolyAI are hospitality, along with banking and healthcare.

Hospitality

Restaurant groups run their entire booking line on PolyAI, and the numbers hold up well:

  • The Melting Pot generated $250,000 from after-hours bookings alone while automating 68% of its reservation calls.
  • Côte Brasserie converts 76% of its booking calls, with 3,000 reservations created every month.

PolyAI also works as the hotel PBX by answering the front-desk line and taking reservations while routing whatever it cannot handle. 

Banking and healthcare

Banking is the second area where PolyAI holds clear ground, for it is backed by a center identity verification that runs on knowledge-based authentication solutions and voice biometrics. 

  • Metro Bank runs its customer line on PolyAI today.
  • In healthcare, its HIPAA compliance and its work with insurers like Simplyhealth make voice AI healthcare deployments a fit it has already proven.

You can choose PolyAI if you’re encountering high-volume routine inbound calls.

Where HappyRobot Wins

HappyRobot is a clear winner if you want to do more than receive and respond to a phone call, which is to drive results in terms of making collections, scheduling carriers, confirming shifts, or generating outbound sales. 

Each call with HappyRobot ends with an action that impacts the bottom line, for example, a booked appointment or a confirmed qualified lead, and how HappyRobot automates operational calls is documented use case by use case. These are also the workloads PolyAI's own reviewers describe as hard to justify under a per-minute CX contract.

Here’s what HappyRobot unlocks.

Outbound revenue work

Sales is the one function where HappyRobot's published numbers measure revenue generated rather than cost saved:

  • Dormant account reactivation returns 28x ROI, with 4.75x more qualified leads generated.
  • Cost per qualified lead drops 75%, and follow-up rate holds at 100% because no lead sits waiting for a rep to find time.

Fast deployment into your existing systems

Forward Deployed Engineers build the first workflows with your team, so going live takes weeks and the phone setup you already pay for stays. Twilio, Telnyx, and SIP lines connect as they are, with no carrier migration and no new telephony contract. 

Other PolyAI Alternatives (2026)

HappyRobot is the strongest PolyAI alternative for operational phone work, and three other platforms are worth knowing depending on how your team builds:

  • Retell AI is built for engineering teams who would rather assemble their own voice agents on an API, and its pay-as-you-go pricing starts at $0.07 per minute.
  • Synthflow takes the opposite route with a no-code builder, so a support or marketing team can launch an agent without writing anything, though it suits smaller call volumes best.
  • Cognigy became part of NICE in 2025 and makes sense when a large enterprise already runs a contact center on Genesys, Avaya, or Cisco and wants AI layered into it.

How to Choose the Right Voice AI Platform

By this point, the split between the platforms is clear, so check your own call data next. Pull last month's call log and sort it two ways:

  • Count the calls that ended with a question answered, and if that’s on the higher end, then PolyAI serves the solution. 
  • Check for calls that created new tasks for human agents such as a payment to chase or an appointment to book. If this is more, then you need HappyRobot.

Further, drill down to implementation and check who maintains the agent after launch. A CX team with no engineers points to a vendor-led platform such as PolyAI or HappyRobot. Conversely, an engineering team that wants control points to Retell AI or Synthflow.

A quick check on five questions to ask before you shortlist one for a demo:

  • In which direction does your call flow: inbound service or outbound operations?
  • Has the vendor published results in your industry?
  • How fast does a workflow reach production, and who builds it?
  • Which of your systems can the agent write into once the call ends?
  • Is pricing per minute, per resolution, or scoped to the deployment?

Final Verdict

There is no single best platform here, only a best fit for the calls you run. PolyAI is excellent for inbound customer service, with its strongest results in hospitality and banking. 

HappyRobot is the best PolyAI alternative when calls have to complete operational work, such as collections, scheduling, and outbound sales, with AI workers live in weeks.
