A major storm at a connecting hub like Chicago O'Hare or Heathrow Airport can cancel hundreds of flights in a few hours. Thousands of passengers would simultaneously demand rebooking. The result is a flood of calls to customer support lines that no human team can clear fast enough.

The best AI automation software for flight ops helps on two fronts: passenger communication and schedule recovery. For passenger communication, voice and messaging agents handle rebooking requests and send status updates via phone, SMS, and chat. Schedule recovery needs software to read the disrupted inventory and rebuild schedules around the seats that are actually available.

HappyRobot operates at the communication layer, handling and resolving passenger contacts during volume spikes. Here, we rank seven tools across both fronts and judge each one on how it performs during real disruption, not just in the demo.

How We Chose the Best AI Automation Software for Flight Ops

We compared the top AI automation software for flight operations feature by feature and ranked each one against the demands of irregular operations. The review is based on each vendor's product documentation and user reviews. 

Five things shaped the ranking:

  • How fast the software responds to a live disruption.
  • How much of the rebooking and reaccommodation is completed on its own?
  • Whether it reaches passengers across voice, SMS, and chat, or just one channel.
  • How deeply it connects to the PSS, GDS, and operational systems an airline already runs.
  • Whether the vendor has verifiable airline use to back its claims.

1. HappyRobot: Best for Automating Rebooking Calls and Disruption Communication

HappyRobot is the best AI automation software for the passenger-communication side of flight ops. The moment a flight changes or is canceled, hundreds of passengers can call within minutes, and HappyRobot answers them all at once with no hold queue.

Inbound call surges are the first thing to overwhelm a call center during irregular operations, and HappyRobot is built to handle exactly that load.

How HappyRobot Works for Airlines

HappyRobot for airlines deploys AI workers across voice, SMS, chat, and email, built on a voice-first agent platform that coordinates each step of the call behind the scenes. Its voice technology is tuned to handle accents, industry jargon, and interruptions, so the agent can be configured to recognize IROPS (Irregular Operations) terminology and how passengers actually phrase a rebooking request.

HappyRobot: Best AI Customer Service Agent for Flight Ops

When schedules change, HappyRobot serves as an AI customer service agent on both sides of the disruption. It places proactive outbound calls and messages to affected passengers before they dial in.

On the inbound side, it determines what each caller needs and handles rebooking without routing them through a legacy IVR menu.

HappyRobot already runs this kind of work at volume in other industries. At Encompass Supply Chain Solutions, its AI workers field roughly 1,700 inbound calls a week and resolve about 64% without a human.

A hub cancellation creates the same problem, in which a flood of calls can arrive at once. Each caller needs to be rebooked and have that booking saved in the airline's system. That is the work HappyRobot already does at scale, so it applies directly to airline disruption. On top of that, you have the live transcript of the conversation. AI workers operate within connected systems rather than only logging a note while responding in 30+ languages.

HappyRobot Integrations

HappyRobot connects to the systems an airline already runs, including its CRM, reservation, and inventory platforms. The way it handles each conversation is configured for the specific job, so a rebooking call and a baggage query do not run on the same generic script.

Credibility

HappyRobot is backed by Y Combinator and Andreessen Horowitz (a16z), and in September 2025, it raised a $44 million in Series B funding led by Base10 Partners, bringing total funding to $62 million. It serves more than 70 enterprise customers across high-volume operations.


Pros: HappyRobot's strength lies in the depth of conversation and its ability to act in real time.
Specifically, HappyRobot:

  • Handles the live rebooking call, as well as chat and email, because it is voice-first.
  • Acts inside your systems during the call, rather than leaving the data entry to your team afterward.
  • Forward Deployed Engineers build the workflows with you, so the system matches how your operation actually runs rather than a generic template.
  • Adapts to your exact workflows instead of forcing your operation into a fixed template.

Downside: HappyRobot uses an AI agent workflow rather than a traditional flight-ops engine, so it works alongside an inventory or recovery system to supply available seats.

It is simply not a packaged aviation product. Forward Deployed Engineers scope and configure the setup to your workflows, so onboarding takes more time and input from your team than flipping on an off-the-shelf tool.

2. Amadeus Passenger Recovery: Best for Amadeus-Stack Airlines

Amadeus Passenger Recovery is the strongest reaccommodation automation for airlines already on the Amadeus stack. When a cancellation or delay occurs, the machine-learning engine identifies every affected passenger and rebuilds their itineraries based on real seat availability across the airline's own flights and other carriers.

Because rebooking runs automatically rather than through an agent for each case, it reduces reaccommodation time from hours of manual work to minutes. The engine also balances recovery against high-yield seat sales, protecting premium revenue and giving operations teams KPI dashboards to compare and refine recovery strategies.

Best fit: Airlines on the Amadeus Altéa Customer Management stack, where the recovery flows run natively.

Pros: Passenger Recovery is mature and proven, though its reach depends on your stack.

  • Proven at scale, with a public, airline-confirmed Air Canada result.
  • Automates the bulk of reaccommodation, up to 90% without an agent.
  • KPI dashboards let operations teams compare and improve recovery strategies.

Cons:

  • You’ll need the Amadeus Altéa stack, so non-Amadeus airlines get limited value.
  • G2 reviewers of Amadeus's broader travel tooling note a steep learning curve lasting up to about 30 days and a weaker mobile UX.

3. Sabre Mosaic Disruption Management: Best for Journey-Aware Reaccommodation

Sabre Mosaic Disruption Management is best for journey-aware, automated reaccommodation across the entire passenger trip. Instead of fixing a single broken segment, it evaluates the entire journey, including connections and available inventory, to build end-to-end rebooking options.

Use Sabre to automatically handle routine disruptions such as delays, cancellations, and aircraft swaps, and route complex cases to agents. Passengers can also self-serve a reaccommodation from their own device.

Mosaic's strength lies in whole-journey logic, though it is a newer platform within the Saber ecosystem.

Best fit: It is built for airlines on the Sabre stack, the SabreSonic passenger service system and the wider SabreMosaic suite. 

Pros: Mosaic's strength is whole-journey logic, though it is a newer platform tied to the Sabre ecosystem

  • Considers connections and the full itinerary, which lowers the risk of stranded passengers.
  • Automates routine reaccommodation and escalates only the complex cases.

Cons:

  • Designed for Sabre-stack airlines, which means if you’re off SabreSonic or SabreMosaic, then there’s only limited value
  • It is a recovery and journey engine, so inbound calls still require a separate voice or contact center layer.

4. Cognigy: Best for Multichannel Rebooking and Refund Automation

Cognigy is best for automating rebooking, refunds, and cancellations across chat and voice at scale. Now part of NiCE, it is a conversational AI platform that airlines use to deflect and resolve high volumes of passenger contacts. You get a low-code flow builder with generative and agentic AI, prebuilt airline templates, and native Amadeus Altéa integration to retrieve PNRs and process rebookings.

Airlines use this tool to handle high volumes of contact during disruptions, as it can automate rebooking, refunds, and cancellations, and even accommodate alternative-flight requests across chat and voice in more than 100 languages.

Pros: Cognigy is strong in multichannel breadth and has proven airline scale, though advanced automation relies on your technical team.

  • Bring voice and chat onto one platform with prebuilt airline templates.
  • Proven at Lufthansa scale during real disruption.
  • A low-code builder lets non-developers maintain common flows.

Cons:

  • Reviewers on G2 note that it does not support very extensive workflows out of the box.
  • Reaching high-level automation requires API, JavaScript, and HTTP skills, so more complex flows fall to engineers.

5. PolyAI: Best for Voice Contact-Center Disruption Handling


PolyAI is best for airlines that want natural-voice automation in a high-volume contact center. It builds voice assistants that answer inbound calls, understand callers across strong accents and background noise, and resolve routine requests without an agent. For travel, it handles FAQs, booking changes, and itinerary questions, then routes a call to a live agent when it gets complex.

Known for the natural voice in its class and reporting call containment above 50% on many deployments, PolyAI runs as a managed service. You can even integrate with existing contact-center stacks such as Amazon Connect and Twilio, so it fits the phone setup an airline already uses.

Pros:

  • Best-in-class voice realism, handling interruptions, accents, and background noise.
  • Strong containment, resolving half or more of routine calls.
  • Integrates with contact center stacks such as Amazon Connect and Twilio.

Cons:

  • It runs as a managed service, so PolyAI builds and changes your call flows rather than your own team.
  • Independent testing measured latency at 700 to 900 milliseconds, among the slowest compared.
  • The focus is on contact-center call handling, so operational rebooking still depends on connected systems.

6. Kaiban: Best for Airlines Wanting Purpose-Built AI Products Across Operations

Kaiban is best for airlines that want AI products tailored to specific airline workflows rather than a single general tool. It is an airline-focused AI company, named to Phocuswire's Hot 25 Travel Startups for 2026, that builds and manages a portfolio of ready-to-deploy products across commercial, passenger care, flight ops, maintenance, and IROPS.

Two products handle disruption directly. Its IROPS re-accommodation product uses AI agents to find optimal alternatives and proactively offer them to affected passengers, and a separate product automates personalized disruption communication. 

Each comes with Kaiban's enterprise layer, including system connectivity via APIs and MCP servers, performance monitoring, and governance, so that the agents run within real airline systems.

Best fit: Airlines that want tailored, department-specific AI products and prefer a deploy-and-manage model over configuring a general platform. Its passenger channels include forms, WhatsApp, chat, and apps.

Pros: Kaiban's strength is airline-specific range, though it is an earlier-stage company.

  • Purpose-built for airlines, with products spanning IROPS, passenger care, and revenue.
  • Each product includes integration, monitoring, and governance, not just an agent.
  • Independent recognition through Phocuswire's 2026 startup list.

Cons:

  • Its disruption communication runs on messaging channels, so a high-volume inbound call surge would need a separate voice layer.
  • Public named-airline deployments are limited as a newer company.

7. Plan3: Best for PSS-Agnostic Disruption Recovery


Plan3 is most suitable for airlines that want automated disruption recovery without committing to one vendor's stack. This passenger-focused disruption platform works with any passenger service system and requires only PNR data to start, so any airline can run on it with minimal integration.

When a disruption hits, its issues dashboard tracks each affected passenger and lets care teams respond to thousands at once using templates.

The platform pulls flight inventory from the Passenger Service System (PSS) and third-party sources, then bundles holistic option packages that go beyond a new flight to include hotels, ground transport, and meal vouchers. 

Best fit: Airlines on any PSS, including those not on Amadeus or Sabre, that want fast deployment and control over recovery cost. Plan3 also partners with Sabre.

Pros: Plan3 is strong on flexible, holistic recovery, though its passenger contact is template-based rather than conversational.

  • PSS-agnostic, with minimal integration to get started.
  • Bundles flights with hotels, transport, and vouchers in one package.
  • Publishes a clear pricing model, which is rare in this category.

Cons:

  • Passenger contact is template-based and self-service, so an inbound call surge still needs a voice layer.
  • Results depend on the business rules the airline configures to prioritize inventory.

How to Choose the Best AI Automation Software for Flight Ops Compares

The seven tools are split into two camps. Some are passenger-communication layers that handle the calls and messages a disruption creates, while others are recovery engines that rebuild schedules and inventory. 

Start by deciding which half of the disruption problem is actually hurting you. If the pain is the flood of passenger calls and messages when a flight goes down, you need a communication layer. If the pain is rebuilding schedules against real seat availability, you need a recovery engine.

Most airlines need both, so the better question is which one you are missing and what your existing stack already covers.

From there, the choice narrows by need:

  • Passenger communication and rebooking calls: HappyRobot, for voice-led handling and proactive outreach at surge volume.
  • Native reaccommodation on an Amadeus or Sabre stack: Amadeus Passenger Recovery or Sabre Mosaic, whichever matches your PSS.
  • Reaccommodation without committing to one stack: Plan3, which works across any PSS.
  • Multichannel deflection across chat and voice: Cognigy.
  • A voice-first contact center: PolyAI.
  • Building your own agentic reaccommodation: Kaiban, if you have the engineering capacity.

A communication layer and a recovery engine solve different halves of the same event. HappyRobot runs the conversations while a recovery engine supplies the inventory, so the two work together rather than replace each other. 

If passenger contact is your bottleneck, see how HappyRobot handles it.

Frequently asked questions

  • Can AI automate flight rebooking and reaccommodation?+
    Yes. Modern tools detect cancellations or delays and identify every affected passenger. You also use it to rebuild itineraries automatically against live seat availability, including seats on other carriers.
  • Does HappyRobot automate airline rebooking calls?+
    Yes. HappyRobot answers inbound rebooking calls at surge volume with no hold queue. It also determines what each caller needs and completes the task within connected systems.
  • How is passenger-communication automation different from a recovery engine?+
    A recovery engine decides which seats and itineraries are available. A communication layer runs the conversation that places the passenger in one. The engine solves inventory, the communication layer solves contact.
  • Can AI handle IROPS during major disruptions?+
    Yes. AI handles thousands of simultaneous passenger contacts during irregular operations, automating rebooking, refunds, and status updates at a volume that a human-only contact center cannot match.
  • What is the best AI automation software for flight ops and rebooking?+
    It depends on the need. HappyRobot is best for passenger communication and rebooking calls. Amadeus and Sabre works well for native reaccommodation on their stacks, and Plan3 for recovery across any PSS.
